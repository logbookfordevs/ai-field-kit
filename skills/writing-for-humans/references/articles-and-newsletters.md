# Articles and Newsletters

Use this reference when the writing must carry an author's voice and sustain a voluntary relationship with the reader. An article earns one reading; a newsletter also teaches the reader what future issues will feel like.

## Editorial Contract

Before restructuring, establish:

- **Central promise:** what worthwhile understanding, feeling, question, or possibility will this piece deliver?
- **Stance:** what does the author believe, suspect, challenge, or remain uncertain about?
- **Relationship:** is the author teaching, thinking alongside the reader, reporting, provoking, confessing, or inviting action?
- **Voice fingerprint:** characteristic diction, sentence movement, density, directness, humor, imagery, vulnerability, technicality, and restraint.
- **Boundaries:** claims requiring support, private material, deliberate ambiguity, and ideas that remain unresolved.

Preserve productive irregularity. A fragment, abrupt turn, unusual image, or repeated phrase may carry personality or emphasis. Improve it only when its effect conflicts with the author contract.

## Build Around Turns

A topic lists what the piece contains. A promise tells the reader why the path is worth taking. Organize substantial prose as **turns**: each section changes the question, model, stakes, evidence, feeling, or available action.

A useful default is:

```text
point of entry -> live question or pressure -> development through evidence and turns -> earned realization -> consequence, invitation, or resonance
```

This is not a mandatory dramatic arc. Reveal the conclusion early when withholding it would tax trust or clarity. Let discovery unfold when the route itself supplies evidence, meaning, or emotional force.

### Opening

- Give the reader a concrete point of entry and enough orientation to recognize the promise.
- Create curiosity by exposing a meaningful gap, not by concealing the subject.
- Begin with scene, claim, question, contradiction, or direct utility according to the piece's actual engine.
- Make the implied contract honest: the body must deliver what the opening teaches the reader to expect.

### Development

- Keep evidence, examples, and lived experience close to the claim they support.
- Move between concrete and abstract so the reader can see both the case and what it means.
- Surface the strongest real objection, alternative, or boundary when it sharpens the idea.
- Use transitions to change the reader's footing, not merely announce another point.
- Let paragraphs vary in length and pressure while keeping every shift legible.

### Ending

- Pay off the central promise rather than summarizing every section mechanically.
- Leave the reader with useful agency: a distinction, action, question, image, decision, or changed way of seeing.
- Use a call to action only when the relationship and piece have earned one.
- For a newsletter, preserve continuity without manufacturing a cliffhanger; the reader should want the next issue because this one respected their attention.

## Edit Without Erasing the Author

Work from meaning outward:

1. **Truth and intention:** identify the actual claim, experience, and uncertainty. Resolve contradictions with the author instead of silently choosing a cleaner position.
2. **Progression:** strengthen the promise, turns, necessary inferences, and payoff. Move or cut material by its effect on the reader journey.
3. **Support:** verify consequential facts, distinguish example from proof, and mark interpretation honestly.
4. **Voice:** restore the author's vocabulary, rhythm, attitude, specificity, and degree of certainty after structural changes.
5. **Line:** remove friction, abstraction, repetition without purpose, and accidental ambiguity. Keep repetition that builds rhythm, relationship, or emphasis.
6. **Read aloud:** listen for cadence, false grandeur, synthetic transitions, and sentences the author would never naturally say.

Never invent personal experience, emotional certainty, sources, quotations, or intimacy on the author's behalf. When a missing detail would provide the piece's human center, ask for it or leave a visible placeholder.

## Newsletter-Specific Continuity

- Orient new readers without making returning readers repeatedly cross the same preamble.
- Carry recurring language, sections, and callbacks only when they strengthen recognition or accumulated meaning.
- Distinguish the issue's immediate promise from the newsletter's broader editorial promise.
- Respect inbox conditions: the subject, preview, opening, headings, links, and closing form one distributed path.
- Optimize for trust and return, not forced completion of every issue.

The branch is complete when the piece sounds like this author, rewards this reader now, and strengthens rather than spends the relationship between them.
